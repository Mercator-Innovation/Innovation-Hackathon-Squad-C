# Personas: Help when it's needed

These people are **made up**. They're built from published research (sources below), not from real people.

## Gary, 46, Inverclyde

- Lives alone in a flat since his marriage ended.
- Uses heroin, and sometimes street benzos (sedative pills).
- His key worker is great, but the service closes at 5pm on Friday.
- Saturday nights are the hardest: he's lonely, he's just been paid, and nobody checks on him.
- His phone is old and often out of data.
- He doesn't trust "the system", and he won't call 999 until it's too late.

## Lorraine, 51, Gary's sister

- Lives 20 minutes away from Gary.
- She's been worried for years, but she's ashamed to tell her friends.
- She doesn't know what she'd do if Gary didn't answer his phone on a Saturday night.
- She isn't sure whether naloxone, a medicine that reverses an opioid overdose, is something she could carry.
- She'd never think of herself as someone who "needs support".

## Aisha, 34, support worker at a local charity

- Has eight years' experience, and is Gary's key worker.
- Works Monday to Friday, 9 to 5. On Friday afternoons she worries about who'll be on their own all weekend.
- On Monday mornings she finds out what happened at the weekend, often from an ambulance referral or, worse, a phone call.
- Her notes are in her charity's system. The NHS, the ambulance service and other charities each have their own, so nobody sees the whole picture.
- She wants people to have someone they trust at night, without having to be on call herself.
- She knows Gary's sister exists, but she can't involve Lorraine without Gary's say-so.

## The moment that matters

**Saturday, 11pm.** Gary's alone and using. Lorraine texts him and gets no reply. Aisha won't know until Monday.

## What the research says

- About 60% of people who died a drug-related death in Scotland lived alone, and 81% died in their own home. ([Public Health Scotland](https://publichealthscotland.scot/publications/national-drug-related-death-database-scotland), via [Drink and Drugs News](https://www.drinkanddrugsnews.com/more-than-60-per-cent-of-people-dying-a-drug-related-death-in-scotland-lived-alone))
- The highest death rate is among people aged 35 to 54. Men's rate is more than double women's. People in the most deprived areas are over 15 times more likely to die. ([National Records of Scotland 2023](https://www.nrscotland.gov.uk/publications/drug-related-deaths-in-scotland-in-2023), [2024](https://nrscotland.gov.uk/publications/drug-related-deaths-in-scotland-2024))
- Ambulance call-outs to overdoses peak on Fridays and Saturdays. ([Society for the Study of Addiction](https://membership.addiction-ssa.org/?p=31874), not checked in full)
- More than 80% of drug support services in Scotland are closed at weekends, according to a charity. ([Salvation Army](https://salvationarmy.org.uk/news/scottish-drug-deaths-reinforce-calls-specialist-support))
- Families waited about 8 years for any support, and only 13% got help within a year. ([Movendi summary of "Ask the Family"](https://movendi.ngo/?p=41570))
- Scottish Families Affected by Alcohol and Drugs says most affected families "remain hidden from sight". ([Scottish Families info sheet](https://www.communitypharmacy.scot.nhs.uk/nhs-grampian/wp-content/uploads/sites/10/Scottish-Familes-Affected-by-Alcohol-and-Drugs-Info.pdf))

**Not found:** Scottish data on what time of night people are most at risk. Aisha's details are my own inference from the challenge sheet, not research.

## Phones and data: what's been tried

- **Digital Lifelines Scotland (2021 to 2023)** gave people who use drugs phones and data. People said it made them feel "more connected with people", and patient, non-judgemental help with setting up mattered. ([Harm Reduction Journal, 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11218389/))
- **The barriers weren't only data.** People feared being tracked or monitored, had low trust in institutions, and lacked confidence with tech. Some preferred face to face, and the authors say a face-to-face option stays "essential". ([same study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11218389/))
- **Funding ran out.** Paying for data after the programme ended was unresolved. The authors call for "a universal data allowance". ([same study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11218389/))
- **The Never Use Alone phone line.** You call before you use and say where you are. If you stop responding, they send help. It was trialled in Glasgow and Ayrshire by We Are With You. We couldn't confirm whether it still runs, or whether there's a text option. ([Transform](https://transformdrugs.org/blog/never-use-alone-overdose-prevention-phone-lines), [Scottish Daily Express](https://www.scottishdailyexpress.co.uk/news/scottish-government-drug-helpline-deaths-25467705))
- **The National Databank** gives free SIMs with data, texts and calls for up to 12 months, through local charities that sign up as partners. We couldn't check which Scottish partners there are now. ([Epping Forest council](https://www.eppingforestdc.gov.uk/free-mobile-data-for-those-who-need-it-most/), [Virgin Media O2](https://news.virginmediao2.co.uk/virgin-media-o2-opens-250-more-national-databanks-and-boosts-free-mobile-data-this-christmas/))

**What this suggests (our inference):** fixing data alone isn't enough. Whatever we build should feel private and not like being watched, and keep a human, face-to-face route open.
